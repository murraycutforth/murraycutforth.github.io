**P1:** We arrive 5 minutes behind another team, classic. Good crevasse stance to rack up on, despite a few ominous cracks disappearing into the snowpack. Nathan leads us up a thin slab.

**P2:** We all stack up on a belay underneath a nasty looking ill-formed pitch of Orion Direct. The chimney of ice had collapsed during the warm spell a few days prior, leaving only the wall to the left as a viable option. Just off-vertical, rippled with ice. It's plenty thick enough but probably not homogeneous enough to protect with ice screws. Fortunately, in this case the Ben provides rock gear at the base of the steep section. Perhaps emboldened by this, I set off before the team above are completely clear of the pitch, and my impatience is rewarded with a hefty chunk of ice to the face, which seemed to be attracting every bit of ice flying past last season. Although I secretly felt like a gnarly bastard climbing the rest of the route with a stream of blood running down my face, it was a reminder not to roll the dice and climb ice underneath other teams, which is a lesson it seems I have to learn the hard way every five years. Ever since being nailed in the face on this pitch I have entertained a fantasy of donning ice hockey shoulder pads, a full face visor, and then climbing Point Five on the busiest day of the season. Anyway, the remainder of the pitch took us to a good ice screw belay underneath the basin - the large snowfield in the centre of the Orion face.

**P3:** Sweet relief - we're out of the Orion Direct crowds. One calf-burning sprint up the basin leads to another ice screw belay.

**P4:** The groove taken by Astral Highway is festooned with icicles. There's a healthy amount of uncertainty over whether it will be grade VI today, but it's more than enough ice for us to arc left up another calf-burning slope to belay under the groove.

**P5:** The real deal. Hannah has been putting a brave face on it, but she's not feeling her best and so is happy for a day in the back seat. Nathan graciously offers me the lead, which I can't say no to. I climb up a bit. Rotten ice, no screws. A bit further. No screws. I go right up to the first overhang, and at the point where I wouldn't go any further without gear, I can get wedged in a groove and plug a cam and a tricam under a rock overlap. Once again, the Ben provides. Buoyed by this, I take the right hand ice line and start plugging away. The pitch is a joy to climb, technical grooves with just enough ice for the odd screw, lead to a steep curtain and an airy belay on my last 3 screws with a view out across the majestic Orion face. There are no guarantees on a big thin face route but today the belays are bombproof.

**P6:** Nathan is back in the lead, up more amenable ice grooves and snowfields to below the 40ft corner on North East Buttress.

**P7:** 40 foot corner - no pushover today.

**P8:** Nathan has constructed a snow anchor, so Hannah and I lead through to the top. Another classic simul lead. Simul leading is too much fun to have any danger associated with it, similarly to glissading.
