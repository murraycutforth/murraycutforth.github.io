Panic rising in the back of my throat, I thrashed through the undergrowth. Sinead was almost out of sight, following a rapidly dwindling, narrow path. The bush pushed in from all angles. Glancing backwards, the beam of my head torch was immediately swallowed up by the dense vegetation. I put my head down and hurried on, pursued by fear and doubt. "I have no memory of this place". Rob's voice was strangely quiet — a whisper in a tomb. Reversing was not an option, but the trail ahead shrank relentlessly. Stooping became crouching, became crawling. Branches slashed my face and grabbed at the ice axes on my rucksack, and soon all hope was lost. If you've been here before then you'll no doubt have instantly recognised this as the Rhododendron forest of Dundonnell, which forms the initial approach to Glas Tholl on An Teallach.

After being forced into a stream bed, which was thankfully almost dry, the three of us emerged onto rolling moorland, and ascended into a steel-grey dawn. The coire itself was silent and timeless, except for a crystal clear burn winding along the basin. The cloud stayed low, and we still saw no sign of the crag. Transitioning from trainers to crampons, we put the final portion of gravitational potential energy in place, and there we were. The Wailing Wall.[^1] The dense mist and full winter coat combined to make it one of the most atmospheric walls in the UK I've ever stood underneath.

The best part about doing a 3 pitch route as a team of three is clear. One pitch each. Sinead led the first, a devious grade VI which ended on a large ledge. At the end of the ledge, a vertical corner provided the access point for the even steeper band looming overhead — the first of two VIII pitches. Rob put in a good solid lead, got the rope up the pitch, and then even better, managed to get the rope back down to us and hauled the bags. Five-star service from Mr. Giddy. Climbing this positive but techy pitch with no bag and a tight rope was an absolute joy. "Are both your axes upside down?" Sinead shouted up, as I gleefully used the new move I had learned the week before on Dark Angel. My confidence was red-hot as I took the sharp end of the rope for the third pitch. Just as well.

After some preliminaries up the side of a vertical tooth, the main difficulties appeared. Repeated quests up and down revealed more gear, but an end to the steep crack system. Eventually it was time to cross the point of no return, and locking off one ice axe I spanned left, hooking a shallow indentation with my feet providing only just enough support from a few millimetres of verglas on the slab below. Another undignified thrutch brought me to a semi-rest and some gear. A few more challenges and delights awaited above, but suffice to say that we got to the top as dusk fell. We were still in the cloud, but the still air lent the entire route an air of unreality. Were we on An Teallach, or was it all just a hallucination created by the embers of consciousness as our bodies wandered the Rhododendron forest?
